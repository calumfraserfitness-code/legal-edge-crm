THE LEGAL EDGE: THREE SHORT VIDEOS
Record approximately 90–120 seconds each. Do not publish until the recordings
are supplied. These drafts do not promise a particular result.

1. MAKING IT WORK WHEN THE WEEK CHANGES
If your routine only works when work is quiet, it is going to be difficult to keep.
A deadline lands. Lunch gets pushed back. The gym session disappears.
The useful question is: what can we still do this week?
With coaching, we agree your normal routine and a smaller version for the weeks
when you have less time. That might mean two shorter sessions, a few repeatable
meals and a realistic movement target. We make those choices together.
Your check-in tells us what actually happened, so the next adjustment has a reason.
The aim is to stop one difficult week turning into another month of starting over.
If we speak again, I would want to look at your busiest weeks, not just your ideal ones.

2. WHY KNOWING WHAT TO DO DOESN'T ALWAYS SOLVE IT
Most of the people I speak with already understand the basics. The difficult part
is carrying them through when the day gets away from you.
If you have tried several plans, I want to understand where each one broke down.
Was it the cooking? The travel? Too many sessions? Or a plan that required every
meal to be perfect?
Then we build around that point. A useful change might be as simple as having a
reliable lunch on office days, or a shorter session you can do when time is tight.
We review whether it worked and adjust it. You get feedback on your actual week,
not another lecture about what a healthy lifestyle should look like.
When we speak again, bring the part that has been hardest to keep going.

3. WHAT COACHING NEEDS TO ADD BEYOND INFORMATION
If you already know a lot about training and food, paying for more information
may not be useful. Coaching has to add something you actually value.
That could mean taking decisions off your plate, spotting why progress has stalled,
or having someone adjust the plan when your circumstances change.
A review should lead to a useful decision. What worked? What needs changing?
What can we simplify? That is different from repeating the same advice every week.
Before we agree anything, we should be clear about what you can already manage
and where you want support. I can then show you an example of how we would work
on that particular part.
If you are happy with your current approach and prefer doing it yourself, that
is a reasonable decision too. Another conversation should be useful to both of us.
